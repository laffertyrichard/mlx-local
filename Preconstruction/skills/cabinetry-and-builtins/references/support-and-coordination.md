# Countertops, shelves and anchoring

## Material-specific top support

Record exact product/family, actual slab thickness (separate from built-up edge appearance), plan shape, supported edges, clear spans, cantilevers, seams, cutouts/remaining ligaments, sink/equipment loads and support attachment. Apply that product's current fabrication/installation guide to the actual geometry and loading, then obtain fabricator/support confirmation. “Quartz,” a nominal thickness and a remembered overhang limit are insufficient.

Corian Quartz distinguishes primary structural support from secondary underlayment; flatness alone does not supply a load path [S11]. Cosentino's Silestone and Dekton guides distinguish configurations with/without cutouts and different thickness/support conditions [S12–S13]. Do not transplant values between these materials or generalize a table into a universal cantilever rule. When a table does not cover the condition, mark that gap and obtain the maker/fabricator's documented resolution.

Removing a cabinet or divider changes the support geometry; rerun the support review even if the countertop outline stays the same. A concealed frame, recessed bracket, rail or different material may preserve an open kneehole, but show how its loads reach verified construction and how its depth fits the body envelope. Do not size steel, certify anchors, or invent an acceptable span from a render. Keep purchase and final fabrication pending the actual product/detail and responsible review.

Carry the accepted detail through installation: assign the responsible installer/reviewer to inspect actual bearing, support locations and connections against it before slab placement. Record the observed result and resolve substitutions, missing attachments or other discrepancies first. An accepted shop detail does not prove it was installed as drawn.

## Shelves, cabinets and TV loads

For shelf performance distinguish clear span from nominal bay width. Record material/grade, thickness, grain orientation, edging/reinforcement, support method, sustained load, creep and acceptable sag; resolve the assembly with its supplier/fabricator or qualified designer. A rendering of a long shelf is not a deflection check. Shorter bays or additional partitions can be useful alternatives, still requiring a real assembly.

Map the complete load/stability path: contents/TV/top → shelf/top/support → carcass joints/rails/base → fixings/substrate → building support. Include TV arm leverage, extended drawers, climbing/tip risk, unit-to-unit connections and floor/base bearing. Thin backs, scribe strips, adjoining cabinets and an anchor icon do not establish capacity. Floor support does not eliminate anti-tip/stability review [S8].

Identify actual wall construction, verified blocking/framing locations, services, stand-offs and compatible mounting system before specifying fixings. Show installation and later inspection/access to connections. Use the tested casework system's installation instructions when applicable [S2]. Unknown substrates or concealed backing are field/qualified-review gates, not a reason to select generic drywall anchors. Peninsula/island support and anchoring need their own substrate/design review; the AWI wall guideline excludes them.

Include a completed-work inspection of case joints, bearing, attachments and TV mounting against the accepted detail before concealment or book/equipment loading. Identify who records it and hold the affected loading/concealment until discrepancies are resolved. Future access for inspection is not evidence that this initial check occurred.

**Source discrepancy:** AWI's public wall anchorage PDF [S2] prints `1406 mm [16 in]` for one horizontal spacing. These units conflict. Do not choose one silently or copy this as a project schedule; seek corrected authoritative guidance and project-specific confirmation. This reference intentionally supplies no default fastener count, spacing or sizing.

## Building interfaces

Coordinate electrical, plumbing, HVAC, vents, fire/escape/access functions and equipment requirements before enclosing them. State the jurisdiction/adopted edition and applicability before calling a dimension a code requirement; use building-code-review when available. Voluntary casework grades, seismic certification programs and aesthetic approval are distinct from project engineering and permits. Preserve unknowns with an owner and a release condition; keep useful conceptual alternatives moving while the dependent fabrication remains provisional.
