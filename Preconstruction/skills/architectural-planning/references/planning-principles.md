# Planning principles

This protocol is synthesized design reasoning, not a quoted standard. See [sources.md](sources.md) for primary guidance and access limits.

## Programming and alternatives

Separate the problem from a favorite solution. Brief: activity, users/frequency/simultaneous use, equipment/storage, privacy/acoustics, daylight/view, adaptability, must-haves and exclusions. Scale the brief to the requested room or project. Use a small adjacency table only where relationships matter; explain conflict pairs such as calls/cooking, guest route/private bedroom or grocery path/hot-work zone.

Define tests: guests reach the toilet without private-room crossings; two people cook without using the main through-route; normal chair pullout works; office calls can coexist with meals. These are brief-specific outcomes, not code dimensions.

Compare retain-and-reconfigure, targeted intervention and larger intervention when relevant. Different colors with unchanged circulation are finish variants, not spatial alternatives. Keep baseline scale/assumptions consistent; state organizing idea, strongest benefit, sacrifice, enabling work and unresolved dependency. Use qualitative or evidence-linked measurements; do not invent weighted precision. Retaining infrastructure can be the best answer.

## Space, views and use

Trace arrival, groceries, guests, laundry, night use and emergency routes; show doors/appliances open and people/furniture in use. Review pinch points, paths through work zones and useful public/shared/private thresholds. Circulation can be valuable; it is not automatically waste or automatically acceptable.

Review clear width/length/height, actual furniture, useful wall runs, openings, visual mass and daylight depth. No obligatory golden ratio or ideal room proportion. Test entry/seated/neighbor sightlines and whether acoustic separation actually closes. More open space can lose storage, wall function, quiet and privacy.

## Environmental choices

Establish true north, hemisphere, climate, season, obstructions, shading, exterior/neighbor levels and occupied times. DOE's passive-solar guide is contextual guidance [S5]; do not transfer northern-hemisphere south-glazing advice to Melbourne or assume Arizona climate for an unidentified location. Distinguish daylight, useful view and solar gains. Test low afternoon sun, direct/reflected glare, cooling demand, operable shading and nighttime privacy. Glazing area is not a reliable comfort score [S5–S6].

Borrowed light may improve perception but does not automatically meet legal daylight, ventilation, escape or separation requirements. High glazing may improve privacy while losing view/escape function. Retain potential escape openings until their role and replacement are resolved. Test shading and sightlines in section, not just in a favorable rendering.

## Three-view test

| View | Resolve together |
|---|---|
| Plan | Boundaries, openings/operation, equipment, occupied routes and support/service zones |
| Section | Levels, headroom, stairs, soffits, structure/service depth, sills/heads, roof/daylight paths and thresholds |
| Elevation | Interior equipment versus sill/head, exterior solid/void/character, neighboring privacy and shading |

Identify contradictory views by source ID and affected decisions. Do not erase a soffit because an elevation omits it, raise floors to meet a roof height, or align facade windows at the expense of function without disclosure. Show needed views at useful scale; full photorealism is not required for concept review.
