# Construction, site fit and finish

## Choose a real assembly

Describe box construction (face frame or frameless), front relationship (inset, full/partial overlay), door construction (slab, frame-and-panel or specified alternative), hardware and exposed ends independently. Shaker is a front language, not proof of face-frame construction. An inset framed reference is not reproduced by putting a Shaker texture on frameless overlay slabs. Identify actual frame rails/stiles and panel members when they are part of the chosen assembly; avoid modeling fake members that imply unsupported fabrication.

| Part | Role and dimension distinction |
|---|---|
| Filler | Deliberate width allowance for operation, spacing or composition; state what clearance or alignment it serves. |
| Scribe | Site-trimmed allowance following an irregular wall/ceiling; record blank and finished exposed dimensions separately. One part may combine filler and scribe roles; count its physical width once. |
| Finished end / applied skin | Finished carcass side or separately applied panel; record thickness, face plane, attachment and exposed return. An applied skin does not establish bearing capacity. |
| Gable / divider / support | A partition may carry load when material, joints, bearing and load path support that role. State its actual role and whether it intrudes into use space. |
| Pilaster / closure / trim | Architectural articulation or concealment; identify whether decorative, operable or structural rather than inferring from appearance. |
| Toe / base / leveling assembly | Recess, usable foot space, support and leveling are distinct functions; coordinate returns, grilles and floor interfaces. |

Do not turn one rejected middle panel into a ban on visible supports. Keep justified divisions, or develop an alternative support outside the needed body envelope with qualified confirmation.

## Fit the site without corrupting the cabinet

Survey width at several heights and depths, wall bows/plumb, floor level, corner angle, ceiling, baseboard, obstructions and services. Keep boxes square/plumb/level; coordinate scribing, approved packing and attachment stand-offs. A single lean dimension does not determine a finished filler. Include assembly/tilting and delivery access, not only final fit.

Reconcile an explicit dimension chain: modules + physical fillers/returns + applied ends + intentional gaps = available run at the stated datum. Distinguish clear interior width from bay outside width and actual stock thickness. Do not double-count a skin included in a module size or a combined filler/scribe allowance. State which end absorbs verified site variation; unexplained residual space is an unresolved dimension, not a fabricated part.

## Grain, edges and moisture

Specify species, solid/veneer/composite substrate, face cut, grain arrows/sequence, matched-front intent, exposed interiors/undersides, edge treatment and finish sheen. Use sample IDs under representative light for color and stain acceptance; “white oak” and a render texture do not guarantee a batch match. Carry matching/sequence requirements into procurement and parts IDs.

Wood moisture and movement depend on species, grain and environment [S7]. Coordinate solid-wood movement and frame/panel or top attachment with the fabricator; avoid rigid cross-grain restraint and invented universal gaps. Select edge banding, adhesives and field-cut/penetration protection for the actual substrate and exposure. Coating an edge does not prove waterproof performance. For baths/laundry, address leaks, condensation, heat and cleaning, including future repair/access.

Specify delivery/storage/acclimation against the selected system and project conditions [S3]; do not substitute a generic room humidity or elapsed time for verified readiness. Plan protection during remaining wet work and maintainable finish/repair details.
