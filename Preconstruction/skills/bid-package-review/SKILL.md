# Bid package review

Help the user determine what was requested, what each vendor actually offered, and what remains unresolved before a bid decision. Follow the user's package and trade scope; do not create a new takeoff or propose unrelated trades unless requested.

## Establish the review context

Use the named project/package, supplied documents, or current app context. Confirm the selected Buildertrend job number, project name/address, and package title before attributing vendors or attachments. The shared Bids URL alone does not identify a project. After switching jobs, wait for the package list to refresh and verify the opened package belongs to that job.

Prefer an available Buildertrend connector; otherwise use supported browser controls in the user's authenticated session. Read current UI labels and links rather than replaying historical element IDs or inventing endpoints. Supplied exports and downloaded documents can support an offline review; say when live response status is unavailable. Ask for the missing project/package only if existing context cannot establish it, and continue any independent document review.

Read the [sanitized workflow summary](references/recorded-workflow.md) for reusable status distinctions. No customer activity-history files are packaged or required. Historical actions do not establish current bid status.

## Review the package and responses

Open the project's Bids page and the relevant package. Capture its title/link or visible identifier, requested scope, release/due dates, open/closed state, attachment count, response count, approval count, and approved total where available.

Expand the package's **All Bids** list or its current equivalent. Inspect all relevant vendor rows, including rows revealed by scrolling or pagination. Preserve vendor names, exact statuses, viewed/submitted/released dates, displayed bid amounts, comments relevant to scope, and vendor attachment links. Count invited vendors separately from submitted responses; a viewed invitation or draft is not a submitted bid. If access or rendering prevents a complete inventory, identify what was inspected and what remains unseen.

Keep these distinctions explicit:

- Package bidding state, vendor response state, and approval/award state are separate fields. A closed package does not prove an award.
- An approved total of $0.00 does not establish the quoted price. Preserve an explicitly displayed vendor $0.00 as a platform value and check the vendor's actual quote before interpreting it.
- A missing amount remains unknown, not zero. If the platform and quote differ, report both with their sources; do not silently replace either.
- Submitted status does not prove a complete, comparable scope. Do not describe a vendor as compliant from its status alone.

## Read attached bid documents

Open the package's attachment list (**View all** or equivalent), then the relevant bid request, plans/details, addenda, and vendor quotes. Track filenames, revision/date, source package/vendor, and page or sheet references. Distinguish a package bid-request attachment from a vendor proposal. Review the actual content; a filename or attachment count proves neither its contents nor that every attachment was inspected.

Use available PDF/document tools for text and visual review. Check diagrams, tables, handwritten notes, and pages whose text extraction is incomplete. Preserve originals and keep downloads or review outputs in the applicable project folder. Do not install converters or dependencies to complete a review. Mark inaccessible or unreadable documents as unreviewed and continue supported work.

Compare each quote with the controlling bid request and applicable revisions. Check the specified work, quantities/units where stated, materials/finish, fabrication versus installation, delivery, inclusions/exclusions, allowances, alternates, taxes, schedule/lead time, validity, and trade interfaces as relevant. Use supplied quantities; any fresh measurement is a separately requested takeoff. Do not assume that a later filename supersedes another document without checking its scope and revision evidence.

Highlight concrete gaps, overlap, outdated revisions, and conflicting statements with citations. For steel island supports, for example, check whether the documents assign field dimensions, anchorage, shop drawings, finish, installation, and coordination with cabinetry/countertops; missing information remains a question rather than an invented requirement.

## Deliver a review the user can act on

Lead with decision-relevant findings. For multiple responses, use a compact comparison table: vendor; status/date; platform amount; document quote amount; scope/revision coverage; exclusions or alternates; unresolved items; source. With one response, give the same information concisely without forcing a table. Keep base bids, allowances, and alternates separate, and show any comparison adjustments as explicit calculations rather than editing quoted prices.

Link findings to the package/vendor record or document filename and page/sheet. State the reviewed project/package, review date, attachment coverage, and unavailable evidence. Separate observed facts from interpretation; recommend a vendor only when requested and when the comparison supports it. Draft specific clarification questions with the conflicting sources and the fact or price needed to resolve them.

Review authorization covers navigation, document inspection, and local review artifacts. Sending invitations, comments, RFIs, vendor messages, submitting/editing bids, or approving/awarding/deleting records requires explicit authorization for that action. Draft communications locally unless sending is authorized; stop once the requested review is complete.
