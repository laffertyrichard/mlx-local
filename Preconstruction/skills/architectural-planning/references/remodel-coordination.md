# Remodel coordination

Use this to identify dependencies in the requested intervention, not to perform unrequested engineering. Source keys are in [sources.md](sources.md).

## Existing-condition basis

Map existing retained, remove, proposed and unknown separately. A visible fixture establishes its visible location, not concealed service route/capacity. Wall construction, bearing status, joists, slab type, reinforcement, foundation capacity and moisture layers remain unestablished without evidence. Request targeted survey of critical widths, levels, ceiling/slopes, opening sill/head/clear operation, visible service entry points and water damage. Avoid assumed door widths, universal stud-to-finish offsets or destructive investigation without qualified authorization.

## Scope-dependent review

| Change | Connected implications and verification |
|---|---|
| Wall/opening/load | Gravity and lateral/uplift path, connections, bearing/foundations, services crossing, temporary support, inspection, protection/restoration. Building America explains continuous load paths [S10]; it is not a member-sizing authorization. |
| Sink/bath/laundry move | Supply/drain elevations, gradients/venting, slab/joist constraints, penetrations, maintenance/removal, floor buildup and accessible levels. A routing corridor is not a sized/designed system. |
| Kitchen opening/soffit/layout | Carry a cooking-ventilation lane for each intervention option: retained/proposed hood and cooking equipment, observed versus unknown duct route/termination, soffit/service conflicts, makeup-air/combustion implications where applicable, required reviewer and estimate treatment. Plumbing drain venting does not close cooking exhaust. Keep the existing system as evidence to verify, not an assumed adequate installation. |
| Curbless/wet area | Drain, slope, floor depth, substrate/structure, compatible membrane/joints/penetrations and transition into other water-exposed areas. Fetch the current chosen system handbook [S13]; never casually notch joists or mix systems. |
| Envelope/window/addition | Continuous conceptual water, air, thermal and climate-appropriate vapor relationships; flashing tied to drainage layer; roof-wall, window, foundation and exterior-door transitions [S11–S12]. No universal vapor-barrier location. |
| Sealing/exhaust/equipment | Ventilation, service clearances, combustion-air and pressure effects. Atmospheric appliances plus stronger exhaust/tighter envelope call for qualified assessment [S7–S8]. |
| Occupied renovation | Temporary facilities, separation, dust/hazards, utilities, deliveries, weather protection, sequencing and future repair. Investigate relevant hazardous-material law; EPA's U.S. pre-1978 lead context is not a universal age rule [S9]. |
| Conversion/heritage/site | Actual current/proposed use, permitted use/occupancy path, fire/egress, sanitation, overlays, access/drainage and significant fabric. NPS guidance is not automatically governing every old house [S17]. |

Keep structural changes, major utilities, uncertain fire/escape, foundations, concealed moisture/hazards and regulatory interpretation visibly dependent on the appropriate professional, field evidence or authority. Do not equate a lower-intervention option with proven safety. Continue supported spatial planning and give concise, answerable questions.

For a kitchen reconfiguration handoff, populate the cooking-ventilation lane even when sink drainage is the most prominent uncertainty. An unknown hood/duct is recorded with its option consequence and mechanical/code-review action; do not drop it under generic “services.”

## Cost and scope

Compare actual intervention drivers, included/excluded/unpriced work, access and related restoration. Do not invent fixed per-area prices or hide unknown work as zero/excluded. Distinguish conceptual allowances from quantities measured from coordinated evidence. Preserve the retained-sink option when island services are unknown; show relocation as a distinct conditional alternate so the owner can weigh its value.
