# Principles for interiors

This is reasoned design guidance, not a technical standard. Use the [source registry](sources.md) to distinguish published advice from synthesis.

## Composition and proportion

Start with the intended experience and organizing idea: gathering, quiet background, artwork/view, axis, material rhythm or deliberate asymmetry. Review scale against room volume and users, hierarchy/rest, alignment of openings/cabinetry/lighting/joints, texture/contrast, coherent transitions, useful negative space, and sightlines from entry and seated positions. Include ceilings, storage faces and service grilles. Explain choices in terms of the brief. Golden ratios, 60/30/10, rug and hanging-height conventions are optional starting heuristics, not evidence-based laws. Respect maximalism, heritage layering and unusual objects when desired.

## Light, color and comfort

Locate tasks and users before arranging light sources. Ambient, task and accent describe functions, not a compulsory three-fixture recipe [S11]. Review distribution, shielding, direct/reflected glare, body shadows, control reach, dimming compatibility, wet ratings and night navigation. Check daylight together with orientation, heat, shading and privacy; review day/dusk/night scenes and standing/seated viewpoints. Avoid a generic downlight grid.

CCT describes source appearance; it does not alone establish rendition of wood, fabric, skin or art. DOE discusses rendition metrics and tunable-source differences [S12–S13]. Do not promise health/circadian outcomes from CCT. IES's public landing page does not expose numerical illuminance tables [S10]; obtain an authorized applicable source before citing a target.

Sample actual finishes at their intended orientation, sheen, adjacent materials and day/night light [S18]. Screen renderings convey intent, not exact paint/material matches.

## Materials, health and assemblies

For each selection record location/exposure, substrate/compatible assembly, wet/slip context, cleaning, heat/stain/scratch sensitivity, repairability/patina, edges/grout/seams/transitions, availability and installed cost basis. Manufacturer details apply to their products and conditions; fetch the current regional installation instructions.

Tile is a finish within a moisture-management assembly [S15–S16]. Coordinate slope, drainage, membrane/joints/penetrations and water-exposed transitions; do not mix incompatible waterproofing systems or assume the shower floor polygon bounds every wet surface. Route technical design to the qualified project team.

Outdoor source-control exhaust is strong guidance; ENERGY STAR criteria do not by themselves establish local law [S17]. Check duct feasibility and ventilation/combustion interactions. Occupied remodels need source/dust control and appropriate hazardous-material procedures when applicable [S14]. Do not treat ordinary dust control as lead/asbestos authorization.

## Acoustics and adaptability

Map noise source, receiver and path. Absorption may reduce reverberation; it does not substitute for separation, sealed paths, doors/services and junction coordination. NRC distinguishes direct and flanking transmission [S19]; a laboratory product STC is not whole-room privacy. Refer demanding targets to an acoustic specialist.

Design access around the user's reach, device, transfer and assistance needs without demanding diagnoses. Plan for change [S8–S9]. Private housing is not automatically within ADA scope; other laws/programs may apply [S6–S7]. A voluntarily chosen accessibility benchmark remains a design target until applicability is verified. Clear/turning spaces have approach and overlap conditions, not arbitrary empty rectangles. Review anchoring and substrate for relevant tall furniture/TVs/appliances with actual product instructions [S23].
