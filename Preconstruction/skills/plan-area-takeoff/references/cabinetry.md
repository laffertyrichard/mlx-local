# Cabinetry

Measure each physical cabinet assembly once, using plan for location/footprint and elevations for widths and operable fronts. A +height note is not a cabinet type or an electrical symbol. Distinguish construction dimensions from door leaves, decorative overlays and appliances.

## LF basis

- Schedule lower, upper and full-height LF separately by room/run. Do not count a full-height tower again as lower and upper.
- State whether the pricing basis is overall assembly width (including specified fillers/end frames) or active storage frontage. If both are useful, retain the second as an alternative with its own label; never sum both.
- Remove appliance/passage openings and unsupported dead corners from cabinet frontage unless the stated basis includes them. At corners, count each actual face once; do not duplicate an intersecting return.
- Count genuinely accessible island storage on each face. Seating overhangs and decorative island panels are not extra lower-cabinet LF.
- Keep open shelving, window benches, exposed decorative panels, appliance panels and passage doors distinct. A planning envelope for storage “to be designed” does not establish cabinet type, fronts or countertop material.

## Front counts

Count operable cabinet door leaves and drawers from visible elevation evidence. Keep a separate list for false/fixed fronts, trash pull-outs, appliance panels and hidden passage doors. Declare whether trash pull-outs are included in drawers; do not count a decorative false drawer above an appliance as storage. Do not infer one pull per front when hardware has not been selected.

Include panel-ready appliance fronts only in the appliance-panel category. A concealed pantry passage remains a passage door, even when its finish matches adjacent cabinets. Record ambiguity between hinged doors, pull-outs and fixed panels instead of forcing a count.

Tie LF and counts to stable run IDs and source view references. Keep cabinet-shop-dependent layouts provisional and record unspecified interiors/accessories without inventing quantities. Do not derive cabinetry quantities from a flooring deduction alone: a footprint can be sufficient to classify supported floor coverage while door counts and shop layout remain unresolved.
