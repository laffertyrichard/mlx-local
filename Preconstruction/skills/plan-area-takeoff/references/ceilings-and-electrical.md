# Reflected ceiling plans and electrical evidence

## Decorative ceiling LF

Identify each run and room on the RCP; calibrate it independently of nearby inset details. For beams, record run count and centerline LF, tracing each physical member once. This is not stock length, wrap perimeter or finish SF. Crown, slats, coffers and tongue-and-groove require their own measurement basis; do not report beam LF as ceiling coverage SF.

Classify new installation, existing retained, repair/touch-up and unresolved scope separately. A specification that retains all beams can make new-install LF zero while measured existing LF remains useful. Do not interpret that zero as zero repair work; establish repair quantity separately. Do not infer ceiling treatment in other rooms from one typical detail unless its applicability is clear.

## Electrical EA and lighting LF

Read the legend and symbol definitions, then count by room and fixture/device type. Identify every counted symbol with a mark or locator and stable ID. Use elevations and furnished plans as supplemental evidence and deduplicate the same fixture across sheets. Distinguish chandeliers, pendants, sconces, recessed fixtures, switches/dimmers, receptacles and other scheduled devices.

Do not interpret counter heights, plumbing controls, door symbols or structural symbols as electrical devices. A note identifying one receptacle supports a note-based count; a general under-cabinet-lighting note without route, segmentation or fixture schedule does not establish fixture EA or length.

If the RCP omits devices or a legend/schedule is missing, report supported partial counts and list the unresolved categories. Never treat “not shown” as zero installed or describe supplemental decorative fixtures as a complete electrical takeoff. Do not derive wiring, circuiting, concealed routing or code-required device counts from architectural evidence alone.
