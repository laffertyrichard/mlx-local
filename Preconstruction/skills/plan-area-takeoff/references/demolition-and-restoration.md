# Demolition, protection and restoration

Use the demolition plan and written scope to establish work. A difference between existing and proposed drawings identifies a question, not an authorized removal. Keep completed demolition, salvage, relocation, disposal and retained protection separate.

## Linked work records

For each in-scope removal, relocation or penetration, record a parent work ID and child rows for the applicable operations below. Give every row a quantity ID or an explicit unresolved/by-others/excluded disposition, evidence, responsibility and question ID. Identify potential related work without automatically including it in the contract.

| Operation | Measurement basis |
|---|---|
| Removal | Material and actual limits; SF, LF, EA or sourced CY; layers and thickness separately |
| Salvage / relocation | Physical item ID, removal and reinstallation operations, destination and condition status |
| Protection | Documented surface/access route SF, barrier LF and supported counts; duration separately if required |
| Sawcutting / access | Actual cut edges LF, openings EA and removal footprint SF; distinguish cut depth from slab thickness |
| Excavation / backfill | Sourced section, route and depth; separate from removed concrete volume |
| Substrate restoration | Concrete, base, reinforcement, vapor barrier, backing, board and insulation as detailed |
| Finish restoration | Actual patch limits and separately specified full-plane blending/refinishing |
| Disconnect / reconnect | Documented equipment or service operation, trade responsibility and verification needs |

Track operations on a relocated item without counting a second physical fixture or new purchase. Removal and replacement are separate operations; a bundled relocation allowance is an alternative to its priced component breakdown, not an additive total.

## Measurement and boundaries

- Trace removal by material and physical layer. Partition footprint SF, wall-face SF and wall LF are distinct bases. Do not use a floor finish polygon as wall demolition geometry.
- Measure actual sawcut edges; a trench adjoining an existing opening may not require four cuts. Do not derive cut LF from every demolition polygon perimeter automatically.
- Where supported, slab removal CY = removal SF × thickness(ft) / 27. This in-place volume does not establish loose debris, weight, truckloads or disposal fees. Density, swell, loading and reuse assumptions need separate evidence.
- Track salvage handling and disposal separately. Do not count the same material as both retained salvage and discarded debris.
- Establish protection extents from the work/access plan or a disclosed provisional assumption. Retained floor area alone does not prove the whole floor receives protection.
- Keep patch footprint separate from blending extent. A small drywall patch can lead to a separately specified full-wall paint operation; overlap across different operations is valid, but overlapping patches in the same layer require deduplication.

For a sink relocation with no service routing, carry the supported relocation count and unresolved connection/access/restoration rows. Do not draw an assumed trench or calculate concrete from the straight-line distance between fixtures. Link the missing route, section, finish extent and responsibility to questions.

Show removal, salvage, protection and restoration with distinct labeled styles. Link access and restoration marks to their originating work IDs. Verify each affected assembly has a disposition for related work; do not infer hazardous-material status, structural temporary works or utility shutdown requirements from ordinary linework.

For demolition coverage, use the occurrence-by-occurrence audit in [irregular-sites-and-callout-audit.md](irregular-sites-and-callout-audit.md). A keynote definition list is not an occurrence inventory; distinguish genuinely unavailable limits from depicted work not yet measured.
