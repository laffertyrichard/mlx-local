# Countertops and slab fabrication

Keep these fabrication groups distinct by room, product and surface ID:

| Group | Quantity basis |
|---|---|
| Countertop horizontal tops | SF of traced/dimensioned top footprint |
| Solid-surface backsplash | SF of vertical slab field, separate from top SF |
| Bench/ledge horizontal tops | SF, separated from ordinary countertop tops |
| Tall aprons, waterfall legs and exposed returns | SF by exposed vertical face |
| Ordinary exposed edge bands | Exposed LF, specified height, resulting face SF if useful |
| Sills and hearths | Top SF when width/depth are known; unresolved SF with established LF otherwise |

Do not collapse these into one fabrication-priced SF. Stone, quartzite, quartz and other slab products can share the reporting workflow but keep their actual material identities. Finished surface SF does not establish slab stock, cutting yield, seam layout or labor rates.

## Tops and backsplashes

Trace L-shaped corners once. Distinguish cabinet bases from top overhangs. State whether tops are gross before sink/cooktop cutouts; keep cutouts and waste separate. Apply an explicitly dimensioned exception to a default overhang, without silently resizing the traced source.

Trace slab backsplash separately from tile backsplash. Deduct tile insets from the slab field. “Full height” can mean counter to upper cabinet/window sill, not floor to ceiling. Read no-sidesplash notes before adding end splashes. A specified pantry counter/splash with no reliable layout has a product assignment but an unknown quantity.

## Floating vanities and other slab assemblies

Inspect plan and every relevant elevation for the top, tall front/apron, exposed ends, returns, waterfall supports and underside. A thick floating slab vanity cannot be represented solely by its horizontal footprint. Keep each established face separate and label unresolved faces; do not assume wall-contact ends or an undetailed underside are finished stone.

For dimensional faces, SF = width(in) x height/depth(in) / 144. For an ordinary edge band, face SF = exposed edge LF x height(in) / 12. Keep the edge LF and height as independent inputs. A finished edge-face quantity may overlap the top's material thickness; it is not automatically extra slab-stock SF. Flat/eased edge wording does not establish mitered fabrication.

Read unusually tall edge dimensions literally when corroborated by elevations; do not silently change a twenty-inch apron into a two-inch edge. A bench side shown in another elevation may be an occluded projection, not an exposed fabrication face. Do not count the same face from multiple views.

Keep a tub-end ledge separate from a nearby open bench. Identify which dimension spans the tub, which projects into the room, and which face is exposed. If later specs revise the size, either adopt that dimensional basis explicitly with conflict/provisional status or retain the drawing with an alternative. Do not combine one face from each incompatible basis without explaining it.

A located but undimensioned sill can have known LF and unknown SF. A spec-sized top may have only a locator on an elevation: mark that geometry `locator_only`, calculate from dimensions, and never use the locator rectangle's SF. If a bench depth changes, flag the linked shower-floor area and wall exclusions for review rather than updating one quantity in isolation.
