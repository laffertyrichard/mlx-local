# Drywall, paint and exterior finishes

## Scope and surface ownership

Build room-by-surface records with new required work, selective repair, retained reference and optional whole-house replacement distinguished. Required new board can be a subset of a whole-house reboard alternative. Declare parent/subset relationships and do not add them. An exposed-to-framing condition does not, by itself, authorize every optional finish in a separate scope document.

Keep substrate surface, material allocation, finish coat and board-layer quantity separate. Gypsum, wet-area backer and fireplace lining may partition a common wall surface; do not assign all of it to each material. Surface SF × layers is material-layer SF, not another installed surface. Missing assembly selections and repair limits stay open.

## Interior walls, ceilings and trim

1. Trace each room's finished wall routes and source its height from plan, section or elevation. Store height breaks, vaults, bays, arches and gables explicitly. Inherited adjacent heights are assumptions. Net wall substrate = route × height + shaped additions - opening deductions; record the deduction policy.
2. Deduct each opening on each measured face it actually affects. Link schedule ID, count, width and height; resolve dimensional conflicts. Trace a shaped arch from an elevation rather than using its rectangular schedule envelope. Treat assumed portal/open passage height separately. Returns, jambs and reveals require their own depth/route evidence.
3. Paint basis starts from the applicable substrate and deducts known tile/slab/wood/other finish areas. Cabinet, backsplash and base allocations may remain unresolved; describe whether surfaces behind cabinets are included. A tile deduction is not a drywall deduction when backer remains required.
4. For new-board baselines, count the actual required faces: exterior walls generally have an interior face, internal partitions can have two, and surrounds may have one. Record the assembly instead of applying two faces to every line. Upper infill, tie-ins, patching and returns remain separate quantities or gaps.
5. Trace ceiling polygons independently of flooring. Flooring under-cabinet deductions do not reduce ceilings. Include or exclude garages/patios from scope explicitly; do not inherit a conditioned-flooring exclusion. For a plane with pitch rise/run, actual SF = horizontal projection × sqrt(1 + (rise/run)^2). Split flat and sloped portions, deduct skylights on the actual surface, and label unknown slopes as projection-only. An established new-ceiling subset is not the whole remodel ceiling.
6. Insulation follows its assembly and thermal boundary. Distinguish exterior walls, ceilings, roof planes and unconditioned garages. Preserve R-values from the source, not a project-independent default.
7. Separate casing LF, base LF, door leaf SF and opening counts. Door casing commonly uses two jambs plus head on specified faces; window perimeter, arches and overhead doors need distinct formulas. State omitted miters, stock allowance, stops and returns. A door schedule can provide dimensional references without proving every leaf receives paint.

## Exterior wall finishes

Trace actual wall returns from the plan and use elevations/sections for heights and openings. Separate new wall stucco, retained stucco paint, patch/tie-in zones and upper gables. A projected elevation face is not the whole exterior envelope. A uniform plate-height quantity is a baseline with upper areas outstanding, not complete stucco.

Allocate stone, wood and other features only where material boundaries are established. Record columns as front/side/return surfaces with explicit depth; do not multiply visible fronts by an assumed number of faces. Keep entry surrounds, window bands, arches, door/garage trim and leaf paint separate. If one finish or surface is already included in a parent area, designate its breakdown as a subset.

## Roofing, soffits and edge lengths

- Store each roof plane's horizontal polygon and pitch. Actual roofing/deck SF derives from that plane; waste and purchase rounding remain separate. Compare roof covering and structural deck boundaries, pitches, overhangs and tie-ins. Preserve differing source quantities as separate alternatives/evidence and flag the difference; do not average or add competing measurements.
- Classify level eaves, sloped rakes, ridges, hips, valleys and existing/new tie-ins. For an edge, actual LF = sqrt(plan_length^2 + elevation_change^2); derive elevation change from the relevant plane and direction, not by applying the plane slope factor to every edge. Count a shared ridge/hip/valley once.
- Measure soffit bands between supported wall/roof boundaries. Test band overlap and containment; horizontal soffit SF and sloped roof SF are different surfaces. Fascia LF does not establish fascia SF without depth.
- Covered patio ceilings require actual coverage evidence. When floor plans/sections show a patio roof but roof plans omit it, keep a conditional quantity and issue rather than silently completing the roof.

## Example of nonadditive scope

If a documented new partition requires 640 SF of lining and the optional whole-house reboard includes that partition in 4,800 SF, carry 640 SF in the required baseline and 4,800 SF as the full alternative. An incremental upgrade is 4,160 SF only after confirming identical surface/layer/deduction bases. Never total 5,440 SF as installed lining.
