# Stairs, railings and specialty metalwork

Reconcile plans, sections, elevations and schedules by physical flight, landing and railing segment. Separate structural construction, applied finishes and fabricated assemblies. Never substitute a projected stair footprint for tread or riser finish quantities.

## Stair ledger

Record flight ID, level pair, source, actual tread/riser counts, widths, tread finish depths, riser heights, shape, finish assignment and quantity IDs. Establish whether the upper landing serves as the final tread; do not assume tread count equals riser count. Floor-to-floor height alone does not establish an approved stair layout.

- Tread SF = sum of each supported finished width × finished depth. Distinguish going from finish depth when nosings overlap; use the assembly's actual dimensions.
- Riser SF = sum of each finished width × riser height. Open risers have no finish face unless a separate detail establishes one. Tread and riser materials may differ.
- Trace winders, curved treads and landings individually. Assign each landing once to the stair or adjoining flooring ledger and retain that ownership through revisions.
- Measure nosing LF along each documented exposed tread edge. Separate stock profile from tread material so an integrated nosing is not purchased twice.
- Record stringer/skirt/cladding faces, underside soffits and trim separately with their slopes, heights and exposed sides. Do not assign finish to concealed surfaces.

## Rails, guards and metalwork

Record assembly ID, function, material, profile/height, location, straight/curved shape, mounting, finish, source and work status. A handrail and guard can be separate components of one assembly; identify bundled and component pricing relationships.

For an established straight segment, actual LF = sqrt(horizontal run² + vertical rise²). Use the actual segment endpoints; floor-to-floor rise may not equal the rise between railing endpoints. Separate level landing segments, returns and specified extensions. For curves, use established three-dimensional geometry or flag length as unresolved; do not scale a foreshortened view as true length.

Count posts, panels, brackets, gates and anchors from depicted occurrences and applicable details. Spacing-based estimates need explicit endpoints, spacing and rounding assumptions and remain allowances. A connection detail alone does not establish occurrence count. Keep infill area separate from individual pickets/cables and their cut lengths.

Do not infer engineered member sizes, welds, fasteners, code-required heights or fabrication cut lists from a conceptual railing elevation. Preserve shop-drawing and field-measurement dependencies. Reconcile railing extents, finishes, new/retained status and responsibility with the design coverage register.
